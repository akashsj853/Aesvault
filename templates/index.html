What is AES?

AES (Advanced Encryption Standard), formerly known as Rijndael, was established by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in 2001. It is a symmetric block cipher that processes 128-bit blocks of data using secret keys of 128, 192, or 256 bits.

  • ✓ Symmetric: same secret key for encryption & decryption
  • ✓ Global gold standard adopted by governments and enterprises
  • ✓ High efficiency in software and hardware implementation
⚡

Why AES-256-GCM?

Standard AES modes like CBC require a separate HMAC to prevent tampering. Galois/Counter Mode (GCM) provides Authenticated Encryption with Associated Data (AEAD), combining confidentiality and cryptographic integrity in one streamlined operation.

  • ✓ 256-bit key offers 2256 possible combinations
  • ✓ Automatically detects altered or corrupted files
  • ✓ Immediate rejection when an incorrect key is attempted
